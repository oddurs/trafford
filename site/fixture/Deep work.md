# Deep work

Notes from a long read, kept as reference rather than as prose. A heading every few lines, which is what reference material looks like once it is written down rather than imagined.

## The premise

The ability to concentrate without distraction on a demanding task is becoming rare at the same time as it is becoming valuable. Those two trends together are the whole argument.

Shallow work is not useless — it is the logistics of a job. The claim is only that it does not compound, and that a career built entirely from it has no floor under it.

## Why it is getting rarer

Open offices, chat that expects an answer inside the hour, and a metric culture that rewards visible busyness over invisible progress. None of these were designed to destroy concentration; each of them does, as a side effect nobody owns.

The tools are not the problem either. A tool that is always open is.

## The four philosophies

**Monastic.** Remove shallow obligations almost entirely. Works for people whose value is a single, clearly identified contribution, and for almost nobody else.

**Bimodal.** Divide time into stretches — a season, a week — that are wholly deep, with the rest left shallow. The unit has to be at least a day; anything shorter never reaches depth.

**Rhythmic.** Make it a habit at the same hour daily. A lower ceiling than bimodal, a much higher floor, and by far the most common among people who actually sustain it.

**Journalistic.** Switch into depth wherever a gap appears. Requires a practised ability to start, and is a poor first strategy.

## Rules that survived

- Decide the philosophy before the schedule, not after.
- Ritualise the start: where, how long, and what counts as done.
- Measure the lead, not the lag.
- Be lazy on purpose — downtime is part of the work, not a gap in it.
- Quit a service only if it fails the craft test: does it help the thing you are actually trying to be good at?

## Measuring it

Hours are the wrong unit and the only one available. Count them anyway, and watch the trend rather than the number: four good hours in a week that had two last week is the signal, and four in a week that had six is also the signal.

Keep the tally somewhere it will be seen. A note is a fine place.

## What it is not

It is not working longer. It is not a productivity system and does not need one. It is not a moral position about anybody else's job.
